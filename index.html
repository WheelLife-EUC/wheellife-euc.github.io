WheelLife — Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 August 2026

WheelLife is a telemetry and ride-recording app for electric unicycles. It reads data your wheel already broadcasts over Bluetooth, records your rides, and — if you choose to sign in — lets you share them with other riders.

This policy describes what the app actually does with your data. Where a setting decides something, the setting is named, along with what it defaults to.

The short version

Your ride routes stay on your phone unless you share a ride. Automatic syncing sends ride statistics only — distance, duration, speeds, battery — never the GPS track.

Anonymous wheel-data capture is off by default on Android. It only ever runs if you switch it on.

Sharing your location with nearby riders is off by default.

There is no advertising, no analytics SDK, and no third-party tracking. Your data is not sold and never has been.

What the app collects, and why

An account, or an anonymous one

The app creates an anonymous account automatically the first time it runs. This is an identifier and nothing more — no name, no email — and it exists so that rides, badges and territory belong to something before you have decided whether to sign up. If you later create a real account, that anonymous one is upgraded in place, so nothing you recorded beforehand is orphaned.

If you create an account, we store your email address and an authentication credential. If you fill in a profile, we store what you enter — display name, city, an avatar image, and details of your wheels. All of that is optional.

Rides

Every ride is recorded on your phone. When you are signed in, finished rides that the app can verify were ridden on a wheel are uploaded as statistics only: distance, duration, average and maximum speed, elevation, battery start and end, temperature, duty cycle, and the start time. This is what leaderboards are built from.

The GPS route is not part of that upload. A route leaves your phone only when you deliberately share a specific ride, and when you do you choose its visibility. A privacy-trim setting can remove a radius around the start and end of the track before it is uploaded, so a shared ride need not begin at your front door.

Location

The app uses precise location while a ride is recording, including in the background via a foreground service with a persistent notification. It is what draws your route and measures your distance.

Location leaves your device in three circumstances, and only these:

  • You share a ride, which uploads its route as described above.
  • You claim territory, which records the coarse map cells you rode through rather than a track.
  • You turn on “Share location with nearby riders”, which is off by default.

Community features

If you use them, the app stores what you would expect a social feature to store: your messages in direct chats, club chats and group-ride chats; comments and kudos you leave; your friendships and club memberships; and group rides you host or attend, including the meeting place you set.

Wheel data capture — opt in

The app can upload captures of the raw Bluetooth traffic from your wheel. These are used to decode wheel protocols correctly: they are how a battery gauge or a speed reading gets verified on a model nobody has tested.

This is off by default and only runs if you turn it on in Settings. When it is on, a capture contains your wheel’s brand, model, name and firmware, the protocol in use, your app version, Android version and phone model, and samples of the wheel’s own data packets. It is tied to your account so a rider can be asked about a specific capture.

Diagnostics

The app records diagnostic notes when something goes wrong or when a ride behaves unexpectedly — for example a ride that fails to sync. These carry a short description and technical values such as distances, durations and error names, plus the app version and device model. They exist to make faults explicable rather than to profile you.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the app registers a push token with Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging so the server can notify you about messages, friend requests and group rides.

Who else processes your data

ServiceWhat it handles
Supabase Hosts the database, authentication and file storage. Everything the app uploads lives here.
Google Firebase Cloud Messaging Delivers push notifications. Receives a device token, not ride data.
Google Maps Draws maps in the app. Map display sends the area being viewed to Google.
Google Play Distributes the app and reports crashes and installs, as it does for any Play app.

No data is sold or shared with advertisers or data brokers, and the app contains no advertising or third-party analytics SDK.

How long data is kept

Rides, profile information and community content are kept until you delete them or delete your account. Diagnostic notes and wheel captures are kept while they are useful for fixing decoding and reliability problems, and are deleted when they are not.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Account → Delete account. This removes your account and the data associated with it from our servers.

By email: if you no longer have the app installed, write to the address below asking for account deletion and include the email address the account uses. We will delete the account and confirm when it is done.

Rides recorded on your phone are stored on the phone as well. Uninstalling the app removes those.

Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have the right to access, correct, export or erase the personal data we hold about you, and to object to or restrict its processing. Write to the address below and we will action it.

Children

WheelLife is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will remove it.
